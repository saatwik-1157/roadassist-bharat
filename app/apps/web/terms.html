It is free and provided as is and as available, with no promise that it works, is accurate, is secure or stays online. 2. No emergency-service guarantee

RoadAssist is not connected to 112, the police, ambulances or any other responder. On the live demo an SOS creates a record and looks up the nearest simulated unit, but contacts nobody: text alerts to your emergency contacts are only written to a log, not sent. Telegram alerts, where switched on, reach only contacts who opted in, and their delivery is not guaranteed. Nothing in the app, including a message that an alert was sent, means that help is coming. Call 112.

3. Accounts

On the live demo, phone sign-in accepts only published demo numbers and shows the sign-in code on screen, so those accounts are shared with anyone who uses the same number. Do not enter real personal details, real emergency contacts or real medical information into them. If you sign in with Google, the account is yours; keep your Google account secure, because anyone who controls it can enter your RoadAssist account. You must be 18 or older to create an account.

5. Mechanics, bookings and payments

On the live demo, the mechanics, service partners, responder units and road-monitoring devices are demo data: simulated accounts and equipment, not real businesses or people. A booking will not bring anyone to you, and quotes, invoices and payments are simulated, so no money moves. We do not vouch for any mechanic, price or repair.

6. AI detections can be wrong

The road scan and the road-monitoring dashboard use computer-vision models to suggest potholes and road damage. They miss real hazards and report ones that are not there. A detection, or the absence of one, is a suggestion, never a statement that a road is safe. Watch the road, not the screen, and never use the app while driving. Map data, addresses, distances, travel times, weather and nearby places come from open data and free services and can also be wrong or out of date.
